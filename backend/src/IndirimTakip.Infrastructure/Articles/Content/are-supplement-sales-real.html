Updated November 9, 2026, with a second measurement. The first one, from October 20, is below for comparison.

On November 9, 2026, two and a half weeks before Black Friday, the US and UK stores we track showed 1,652 supplements with a crossed-out "was" price. We checked each one against what the product actually sold for in the month before its current price appeared. In US stores, 24 of the 429 products we could judge were really cheaper, half as many as in October. In UK stores it was 486 of 1,014, up from 11 of 692: Myprotein started a sale on November 2, and its prices really did drop, though by much less than its labels say.

How we checked

A crossed-out price only tells you what the store says the product used to cost. We record every product's price several times a day, so we can see what it actually cost. The rule:

  • The reference price is the lowest price the product had in the 30 days before its current price started. A price has to appear on at least two different days to count, so a one-day glitch doesn't set the bar.
  • Really cheaper means today's price is below that reference.
  • Same price for a month: the price hasn't moved in more than 30 days, yet the "was" price is still shown. By then the sale price is simply the price.
  • Not cheaper: in the 30 days before the current price started, the product sold for the same or less on at least two days.
  • Not counted: we need our own price checks on at least 7 different days in those 30 days. New products, products that were out of stock and stores we added recently often fall short, and we'd rather leave them out than guess.

This is the rule the EU has applied to "was" prices since 2022. It isn't the legal test in the US or the UK; we use it because it's simple, and a store can only pass it by having actually charged more. We measure the price on the product page, so coupon codes and cart discounts aren't included. More on how we collect prices: how it works.

The results

US stores, 512 products with a crossed-out price:

  • Really cheaper: 24, or 6% of the 429 we could judge
  • Same price for over a month: 373
  • Not cheaper: 32
  • Not counted, too little price history: 83

UK stores, 1,140 products with a crossed-out price:

  • Really cheaper: 486, or 48% of the 1,014 we could judge
  • Same price for over a month: 460
  • Not cheaper: 68
  • Not counted, too little price history: 126

What changed since October 20

On October 20, US stores showed 610 crossed-out prices: 49 of the 409 we could judge were really cheaper (12%), 227 had been at the same price for over a month and 133 were not cheaper. UK stores showed 1,078: 11 of 692 were really cheaper (under 2%), 327 had been at the same price for over a month and 354 were not cheaper.

In the US, the sales that had sat at one price kept sitting: products at the same price for over a month went from 227 to 373, and the real discounts halved, from 49 to 24. In the UK, the current prices of 308 Myprotein products began on November 2, as did those of its sister stores Myvitamins and Myvegan, and Grenade followed on November 8. Real discounts in UK stores went from 11 to 486, and 345 of them are Myprotein's; products not cheaper than the month before fell from 354 to 68.

The biggest group in the US: "on sale" for over a month

In US stores, 373 of the 429 products we could judge had kept the same price for more than 30 days while still showing a crossed-out price. In UK stores it was 460 of 1,014. The typical claimed saving on these was 25% in the US and 33% in the UK (medians), the same as in October.

Some categories are mostly this. In US stores, 27 of the 33 creatine products with a crossed-out price, 25 of the 29 hydration products, 71 of the 97 pre-workouts and all 26 vitamins had been at the same price for over a month. In UK stores it was 73 of 115 creatine products and 86 of 116 amino acid products.

Not cheaper than the month before

32 US products and 68 UK products were "on sale" at a price they had already sold at, or below, on at least two days in the previous 30. That is what a sale that switches on and off looks like, or a price that went up shortly before the discount. On these, stores claimed a median of 10% off in the US and 41% in the UK.

Where the real discounts were

The 24 real discounts in US stores were a median of 23% below the reference price, while the stores claimed a median of 40% off on the same products. RAW Nutrition, Bodybuilding.com Signature and Redcon1 had 6 each. The biggest were still the two Nutricost products from October, NutriZen Focus and NutriZen Unwind: $8.39, against a lowest price of $27.97 in the month before, 70% less.

The 486 in UK stores were a median of 14% below the reference, while the stores claimed a median of 40% off on the same products. Protein powders had the most (244), then protein snacks (58). The biggest was MyPRO THE Electro, a HYROX electrolyte powder (60 servings), at £15.00, against £35.99 in the month before: a real 58% drop, though the store's label says 67%.

What to do this Black Friday

  • Ignore the crossed-out price. Look at what the product sold for in the last few weeks; our product pages show the full price history.
  • Be wary of a sale that never ends. If a product has been "on sale" for a month, that's its price.
  • A real drop can still be smaller than the label. In UK stores this November, the typical real drop was 14% where the label said 40%.
  • Compare what you get, not the tub price. Cost per serving or per gram tells you more than a percentage off; our creatine dosage calculator works out how long a tub lasts and what it costs per day.
  • Check our Black Friday page. It runs the same test on current prices and lists only the products that pass.
